### 1. Retrieval practice
**Key concept:** Retrieval practice means attempting recall before checking the source.
**Explanation:** In the fictional study, students tried to remember the information with their notes closed.
**Example:** Students closed their notes and wrote what they remembered.

### 2. Spaced practice
**Key concept:** Spaced practice separates study sessions over time.
**Explanation:** The fictional study placed its sessions on different days.
**Example:** Sessions took place on Monday and Thursday.

### 3. Feedback on recall
**Key concept:** Feedback identifies what learners recalled incorrectly.
**Explanation:** Students checked their answers after each recall attempt.
**Example:** Students compared their answers with the lesson notes.

### 4. Transfer to a different task
**Key concept:** Transfer means applying learning to a different task.
**Explanation:** The fictional study assessed use of a taught rule on a new diagram.
**Example:** Students applied the taught rule to the new diagram.

### 5. A finding with limited causal evidence
**Key concept:** The study's score difference does not establish that retrieval practice caused it.
**Explanation:** Retrieval-practice students scored 12 percentage points higher on the final quiz. The study included only 24 students from one class, and the author notes that students chose their own groups, so the result does not establish causation.
**Self-test:** Why does the author say the difference does not establish causation? Answer: Students chose their own groups.
